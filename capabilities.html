Tactics — TLC every day

Plans look great on paper, but can stumble on execution. And mostly, it's not because the plans themselves were bad. Plans fail because no one owns all of it.

Briefs get handed off. Accountability gets distributed. And results end up belonging to no one's brief in particular.

This becomes an even bigger problem when vendors come into the picture. Not only does it fragment things further, vendors rarely take ownership of results. Number of activities — sure, but not what really matters. Engagement. Revenue.

On the other hand, we own the execution, from brief to result. And we can do that because it's one team. One point of accountability. When there are no gaps, nothing slips through.

Analytics — For a more verdant garden

Most marketing reports look the same. Clicks, impressions, reach. Impressive numbers, but what do they mean? And how are they impacting the most important number of all — revenue?

The truth is, no one knows. Because these are measuring activity, not impact. Without a proper framework that ties everything together, from first touch to closed deal, these reports are at best, a distraction.

We build our metrics framework from the planning stage — nailing down attribution and identifying signals that align with revenue. This means we don't simply report clicks, impressions and reach and call it a day. We know the metrics that matter to the business.

This is powerful knowledge that helps us identify what works, and what can be cycled out. After all, when the correct numbers are tracked, the decision making becomes easy.
